# Standards, applicability and citation

Choose the project-controlled edition and tailoring before reviewing against a standard.
Do not substitute the latest edition merely because it exists. Record the document ID,
edition/change, exact inspected clause/page, approved applicability, and source. A source
that only supplies the title or scope supports identification, not a clause-level finding.
Never invent a clause number, normative quotation, objective, or project approval.

## Source register

These are authoritative discovery links recorded on 1 October 2026. The full standard is not redistributed here.

- [ECSS-E-ST-10-06C, Technical requirements specification, 6 March 2009](https://ecss.nl/standard/ecss-e-st-10-06c-technical-requirements-specification/)
  identifies the standard for technical requirements. It allows project tailoring; the
  controlled text must be inspected before clause-specific findings.
- [ECSS active engineering standards](https://ecss.nl/standards/active-standards/engineering/)
  is a discovery index. Includes verification E-ST-10-02, testing E-ST-10-03,
  system engineering E-ST-10 and interface management E-ST-10-24. Select the agreed edition.
- [ECSS active standards](https://ecss.nl/standards/active-standards/) identifies
  management M-ST-40 and product assurance Q-ST-30/Q-ST-40 and software Q-ST-80 families.
- [NASA Systems Engineering Handbook, SP-2016-6105 Rev.2](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)
  supplies lifecycle, requirements, design, verification and technical-management guidance.
- [NASA NODIS](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/) identifies controlled NPR 7123.1 and
  NPR 7150.2 directives. Verify edition and change actually served; do not infer it from a URL.
- [NASA-STD-8739.8](https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/nasa/nasa-std-87398)
  identifies software assurance and software safety requirements.
- [INCOSE publications](https://www.incose.org/publications) identifies the Systems
  Engineering Handbook and Guide for Writing Requirements. Licensed text is not bundled.
- [FAA AC 20-174](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_20-174.pdf)
  addresses use of ARP4754A for civil-aircraft development assurance. FDAL and IDAL
  allocation rationale is distinct from software level and hardware DAL.
- [FAA AC 20-115D](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_20-115D.pdf)
  addresses airborne software assurance using DO-178C and supplements.
- [FAA AC 20-152A](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_20-152A.pdf)
  addresses airborne electronic hardware assurance using DO-254; it is not a generic
  mechanical hardware standard.
- [FAA AC 21-16G](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_21-16G.pdf)
  addresses use of environmental qualification references such as DO-160G.
- [SAE standards](https://www.sae.org/standards) and [RTCA](https://www.rtca.org/)
  are publishers of licensed aviation standards. ARP4754B does not silently replace
  ARP4754A in an agreed certification basis; ARP4761A supports aircraft safety assessment.

## Review procedure

Build a narrow applicability record: obligation/objective, applicable item or process,
edition, contractual/authority basis, tailoring rationale and decision status, required
evidence, evidence supplied, finding. Distinguish normative “shall” criteria from
handbook suggestions and Toolkit-authored heuristics. A newer standard, a supplied marketing
claim, or a passing test does not by itself change the compliance basis.

If text is unavailable, deliver an advisory engineering review with explicit unsupported
standard checks. Do not label a subject compliant, certified, or noncompliant on the basis
of a summary. When user context supplies excerpts, retain their declared identity and
locator; identify any unverified provenance without rejecting useful technical analysis.
Keep quotations limited to the amount necessary to explain a finding; prefer original
analysis and clause locators over reproducing manuals.

## Model distinctions

Aircraft FDAL, IDAL, software level and hardware DAL are not interchangeable. NASA
software class and ECSS criticality are different schemes. Do not inherit a parent's
level without the applicable allocation process, equate severity with DAL, or infer
independence objectives from memory. Verification, validation, compliance and certification
answer different questions and require different evidence and approval.
